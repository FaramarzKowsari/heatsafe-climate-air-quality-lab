# Scientific Vision

## Purpose

HeatSafe is an independent environmental-intelligence research platform. Its purpose is to make compound heat and air-quality research reproducible, inspectable and useful across climate science, environmental health, urban analytics, machine learning and indoor resilience.

## Core research proposition

Environmental risk is not a single-variable problem. Heat, humidity, smoke, PM2.5, nighttime heat retention, urban form and indoor thermal response interact across time and space. HeatSafe therefore treats each data product as a typed observation with provenance and evaluates both individual hazards and compound conditions.

## Non-negotiable scientific principles

1. Deterministic or statistical results remain primary.
2. AI-generated language cannot replace measurements or decisions.
3. Forecasts, observations, reanalysis, satellite products, estimates and synthetic data remain distinct.
4. Time-series evaluation must avoid random temporal leakage.
5. Geospatial evaluation must test transfer to unseen places.
6. Uncertainty must be measured and reported.
7. Baselines must be competitive and reproducible.
8. Negative and inconclusive findings are retained.
9. External data licenses and redistribution limits are respected.
10. Every public claim must be traceable to code, data, configuration and a versioned artifact.

## Research tracks

### Compound environmental risk

Develop transparent methods for co-occurring heat, PM2.5, smoke, humidity and hot-night conditions. Compare additive, interaction-aware and event-based formulations; quantify sensitivity to weights and thresholds; avoid presenting exploratory indices as validated health scores.

### Forecasting and uncertainty

Benchmark persistence, seasonal-naive, linear, tree-based, neural and probabilistic methods using chronological, rolling-origin and domain-shift splits. Report point accuracy, event detection, calibration, interval coverage and interval width.

### Geospatial generalization

Evaluate city-aware and region-aware models using spatial cross-validation, leave-one-city-out and leave-one-region-out protocols. Examine transfer across Europe, North America and Türkiye.

### Urban heat and remote sensing

Integrate land-surface temperature, vegetation, built-form, elevation and station data while preserving the distinction between satellite surface temperature and near-surface air temperature.

### Wildfire smoke context

Combine fire detections, distance, wind plausibility and PM2.5 changes without claiming causal source attribution unless a dedicated transport model and validation support it.

### Indoor resilience

Study heat retention, ventilation opportunity, cooling demand, thermal lag and user-entered household observations. Keep these tools separate from official building certification and medical guidance.

### Accessible and advanced AI

Maintain a complete no-paid-API path. Add local open-source models and optional BYOK providers only as explanation, coding, retrieval or research assistants with explicit grounding and provenance controls.

## Target scientific outputs

- HeatAQ Nexus benchmark releases
- multi-region versioned datasets
- experiment manifests and reproducibility bundles
- model cards and data cards
- technical reports and preprints
- a reviewed research-software release with DOI
- a software paper after independent review
